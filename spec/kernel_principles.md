# Kernel Principles

Kernel Core exists to preserve judgment integrity.

Its purpose is not to determine truth, select actions, or enforce policy.

Its purpose is to ensure that judgments remain structurally valid, evidence-bound, traceable, and uncertainty-aware before they are consumed by downstream systems.

---

## Principle 1 — Evidence First

All interpretations must remain linked to observable evidence.

No claim may be introduced without an identifiable evidence path.

Evidence is the foundation of every valid judgment.

---

## Principle 2 — Uncertainty Preservation

Uncertainty is information.

When evidence is incomplete, conflicting, ambiguous, or weak, uncertainty must remain visible rather than being collapsed into a single interpretation.

The Kernel preserves uncertainty instead of resolving it.

---

## Principle 3 — Traceability Required

Every judgment must maintain a complete traceability chain.

Observation → Evidence → Interpretation → Cause Assignment

If this chain is broken, the judgment event is structurally invalid.

---

## Principle 4 — One Event, One Primary Cause

A judgment event may contain multiple contributing factors.

However, exactly one primary cause must be assigned.

This constraint prevents causal inflation and preserves analytical consistency.

---

## Principle 5 — No Hidden Inference

The Kernel must not introduce information that is not explicitly supported by evidence.

Assumptions, contextual guesses, and inferred background knowledge are outside the Kernel boundary.

---

## Principle 6 — Weaker Interpretation Preferred

When multiple interpretations are possible, the interpretation requiring fewer unsupported assumptions should be preferred.

The Kernel favors structural conservatism over confident reconstruction.

---

## Principle 7 — Structural Integrity Over Fluency

A fluent judgment is not necessarily a valid judgment.

Structural correctness takes priority over readability, completeness, or narrative coherence.

---

## Principle 8 — Deterministic Processing

Identical inputs must produce identical outputs.

Kernel behavior must be reproducible, auditable, and independent of evaluator preference.

---

## Principle 9 — Boundary Preservation

The Kernel does not:

* assign STOP / DEFER / ALLOW
* apply policy rules
* execute governance logic
* perform audits
* infer hidden context
* select repair operators

These responsibilities belong to downstream systems.

---

## Principle 10 — Judgment Before Governance

The Kernel validates structure.

Downstream systems determine meaning, policy, escalation, and action.

The Kernel exists to ensure that only structurally valid judgment events can propagate beyond the observation layer.

---

## Summary

Kernel Core is not a decision system.

Kernel Core is a structural enforcement layer that preserves evidence, uncertainty, traceability, and causal integrity.

Its role is to prevent invalid judgment structures from entering downstream governance systems.
